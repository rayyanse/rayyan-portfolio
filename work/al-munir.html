Home/Work/Al Munir UPVC
Client build · manufacturing

A sales pipeline that stays open after the lights go off.

Al Munir makes UPVC windows in Lahore. Their office closes at 6pm, their customers don't. This pipeline finds buyers in Facebook groups, calls them with a voice agent and sends a priced PDF quote on WhatsApp. Nobody touches a calculator.

The problem

Someone comments “price?” on a Facebook post at 9pm. The team sees it at 9am. By then that person has bought windows from someone else.

That gap was the whole problem. Not marketing. Not pricing. Office hours.

How it works

Step by step

  1. Find buying intent

    Apify scans Facebook groups and Instagram hashtags for people asking about windows. Real leads land in a Google Sheet within minutes.

  2. First message, straight away

    UltraMsg sends the first WhatsApp message automatically.

  3. A voice agent calls

    A Retell voice agent calls the lead, asks what they need and logs the outcome.

  4. A priced quote on WhatsApp

    If the call goes well, a quote is priced and sent as a two-page PDF built from the company's own branded template.

  5. Visits book themselves

    A measurement visit goes into Google Calendar and the team gets an email, so it never gets forgotten.

  6. DMs answered in Roman Urdu

    On Instagram, a Claude sales bot collects sizes and location in Roman Urdu, and knows not to quote prices.

Under the hood

The decisions that mattered

  • A person takes over when asked

    The moment a customer asks for a human, the conversation hands over to the team.

  • Their quote, not a generic one

    Quotes render from Al Munir's own branded template as a two-page PDF, so a midnight quote looks like one from the office.

  • Scheduled jobs plus webhooks

    Schedulers run the scraping and calling on-site; anything inbound hits serverless webhooks on Vercel.
